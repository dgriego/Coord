Decisions made

What's settled

D1 · Accounting

QuickBooks owns the books. We make the documents and sync them over.

D2 · Approvals

No formal submittal log. A light approval step for CFAs, shop drawings and slabs.

D3 · US only

USD, US sales tax, money stored in cents.

D4 · Payments

Stripe Connect. Studios get paid directly; we never hold funds.

D5 · Receiving

Warehouse receiving and damage claims are in. Inventory accounting is out.

D6 · Share links

Clients, vendors and contractors use secure links, with no accounts.

D7 · Simplicity

Everything included, no add-on modules. Advanced features appear only when turned on.

D8 · Design tools

Integrate with mood-board and floor-plan tools. We only build the product clipper.

Core idea

Enter an item once, use it everywhere

Every item feeds the client proposal, the vendor POs and the contractor schedule. Sent documents keep a copy as of the day they went out, so later edits never change them.

Item lifecycle

From draft to installed

Each stage is set by actions in the app, not typed in. An item is only as far along as its slowest part, so the sofa isn't "shipped" until both POs ship.

Draft→ Proposed→ Approved→ Deposit paid→ Ordered→ Acknowledged→ In production→ Shipped→ Received→ Delivered→ Installed
Side paths: RejectedBackorderedDamagedCancelled · An open approval (CFA, shop drawing) blocks ordering.

Payments compliance

What taking payments requires

Card security (PCI)

Card numbers stay inside Stripe's fields and never reach our servers.

Money transmission

Each studio is paid directly through Stripe Connect, so we never hold client funds.

Identity and tax forms

Stripe verifies each studio and issues 1099-K forms.

Card fees to clients

Capped by card networks and restricted in some states. Off by default; confirm rules with counsel.

ACH timing

Bank payments take days to settle. Shown as "processing" until they clear.

Disputes

Signed proposals and approvals are kept as evidence for chargebacks.

For people new to the trade

Interior design glossary

The words designers, vendors and contractors use every day, in plain language. Green notes say where each one shows up in the app.

Process

Consultation
The first paid meeting, where the designer learns the client's goals, budget, timeline and style, and walks the space.
Programming
Writing down what each room must do and hold: who uses it, what furniture it needs, storage, lighting. Usually part of the consultation phase.
Schematic designSD
The early concept phase: rough layouts, mood boards and overall direction, before specific products are chosen.
Design developmentDD
Turning the concept into real choices: exact furniture, fabrics, finishes, fixtures and their sizes. Most proposals are built here.In the app: the phase where items are added to rooms.
Construction documentsCD
The finished drawings and schedules a contractor builds from.
Construction administrationCA
The designer's work while building happens: site visits, answering contractor questions, checking work matches the plans.
FF&E
Furniture, fixtures and equipment: the movable items in a project, as opposed to built-in construction.
Scope of work
The written list of what the designer will and won't do on a project. It sets what the fee covers.
Design agreement
The contract between designer and client covering scope, fees, deposits and terms. Also called a letter of agreement.
Site visit
A trip to the project to check progress, measure or meet trades.
Punch list
The list of small fixes left at the end of a job: a scratched table, a missing outlet cover, a crooked fixture.
Install day
The day furniture and accessories are delivered and placed, often staged so the client sees the room finished all at once. Also called a reveal.
Change order
A written change to work already agreed, usually with a new price or date, that the client signs.

Products and materials

Casegoods
Furniture made from hard materials with no upholstery: dressers, desks, nightstands, bookcases, dining tables.
Upholstery
Furniture covered in fabric or leather over padding: sofas, chairs, ottomans, headboards.
Soft goods
Fabric items made for a project: drapery, roman shades, pillows, bedding.
Workroom
A custom shop that builds upholstery, drapery or other soft goods to the designer's specifications.
Millwork
Custom woodwork built for the space: cabinets, built-in shelving, paneling, trim.
To the trade
Sold only to design professionals, not the public. Many high-end showrooms and vendors work this way.
Showroom
A vendor's display space where designers see and order products, often to the trade only.
Customer's own materialCOM
Fabric bought separately and shipped to the furniture maker to cover a piece. A custom sofa in COM fabric involves two vendors and two orders.In the app: one item with two parts that creates two purchase orders.
Customer's own leatherCOL
The same idea as COM, with leather instead of fabric.
Yardage
How many yards of fabric a piece needs. Furniture makers publish the yardage for each style.
Pattern repeat
The distance before a fabric's pattern repeats. Large repeats need extra yardage so the pattern lines up.
Railroaded
Fabric whose pattern runs along its length instead of across, so a long sofa can be covered without seams.
Double rubs
A measure of how well fabric resists wear. About 15,000 or more is typical for home seating.
Memo sample
A small cutting of fabric or material sent so the designer can see and feel it before ordering.
Strike-off
A test print of a custom fabric or wallcovering, sent to approve color before the full run is made.
Slab
One large piece of natural stone. Designers often choose the exact slab at the stone yard because every one looks different.
Drawdown
A paint sample brushed onto a card or wall to check the real color before painting.

Ordering and delivery

Purchase orderPO
The document a designer sends a vendor saying exactly what to make or ship: item, size, finish, fabric, quantity, price and delivery address.In the app: created from approved items, one per vendor.
Acknowledgment
The vendor's reply confirming a PO: what they'll make, the price and the expected ship date. Errors get caught here.
Cutting for approvalCFA
A small piece cut from the actual bolt of fabric for an order, sent to confirm color before the whole length is cut. Dye lots vary.In the app: an approval that blocks ordering until it's signed off.
Lead time
How long a vendor needs to make and ship an item. Custom pieces often take 8–16 weeks or longer.
Backorder
An item that's out of stock, so it ships later than promised.
Sidemark
The client or project name put on an order and its boxes so the warehouse knows whose it is.
Freight
The cost of shipping large items, usually by truck. Often charged separately from the product.
Crating
A wooden crate built around a fragile or valuable piece for shipping, with its own fee.
Receiving warehouse
A company that accepts deliveries for designers, inspects them for damage and stores them until install day. Also called a receiver.In the app: receiving records with photos and damage notes.
White-glove delivery
Delivery that includes bringing items inside, unpacking, assembling, placing them and taking the packaging away.
Damage claim
A request to the vendor or carrier to repair or replace something that arrived damaged.
Drop ship
When a vendor ships straight to the client's home instead of to the designer or warehouse.

Documents and drawings

Proposal
The document listing the items a designer recommends, with prices for the client to approve and pay a deposit on.In the app: sent as a link where the client approves and pays.
Schedule
A table telling the contractor exactly what goes where: every light fixture, plumbing fitting, tile or paint color, with model numbers and locations.In the app: lighting, plumbing and finish schedules with revisions.
Tag
A short code like L-1 or P-3 that links an item on a schedule to its spot on a drawing.
Specification
The full description of a product: maker, model, size, finish and options. Often shortened to spec, as in "spec a fixture".
Tear sheet
A one-page product sheet from a vendor with a photo, dimensions and options. Also called a cut sheet.
Floor plan
A drawing of a room from above showing walls, doors and furniture layout.
Elevation
A flat drawing of one wall seen straight on, showing heights of cabinets, art, sconces and trim.
Reflected ceiling planRCP
A drawing of the ceiling showing where lights, fans and vents go.
Shop drawing
A detailed drawing from a maker (like a millwork shop) showing exactly how they'll build a custom piece, sent for the designer's approval.
Submittal
On commercial jobs, the formal package of shop drawings, product data and samples a contractor sends for approval. Residential designers do the same thing less formally.
Mood board
A collage of images, colors and materials that shows the feeling of a design before specific products are chosen.
Revision
An updated version of a drawing or schedule. Changes are marked so the contractor can see what's different.

Money and billing

Net price
What the designer pays a vendor, after their trade discount. Also called trade price or cost.
MSRP
The manufacturer's suggested retail price: what the public would pay. Also called list price or retail.
Trade discount
The percentage off retail that vendors give designers, often 15–50%.
Markup
The amount added on top of cost to set the client's price. A 30% markup on a $1,000 lamp makes it $1,300.
Margin
The share of the selling price that's profit. Selling a $1,000 lamp for $1,300 is a 23% margin. Mixing up markup and margin is a common pricing mistake.
Flat fee
One agreed price for the design work, regardless of hours spent.
Hourly billing
Charging for design time by the hour, tracked and invoiced regularly.In the app: timers and time entries that flow onto invoices.
Cost-plus
Billing the client the designer's cost for products plus an agreed percentage.
Retainer
Money paid up front before work begins, then used up against later invoices.
Deposit
A part payment before an order is placed. Clients often pay 50–100% up front for custom items, and designers pay vendors around 50%.
Reimbursables
Out-of-pocket costs billed back to the client, such as mileage, printing, samples or travel.
Resale certificate
A state form that lets a designer buy products without paying sales tax, because tax is collected when they resell to the client.
Net 30
Payment terms meaning the invoice is due 30 days after it's issued.

No terms match. Try a shorter word.
